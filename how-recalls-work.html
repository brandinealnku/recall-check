How RecallCheck works

Scan. Compare. Verify.

RecallCheck helps you compare a packaged food with available FDA and USDA recall records. The official recall notice remains the final source for package-specific instructions.

  1. 1

    Scan or enter the barcode

    Use the barcode printed on the package. If you do not have the package, you can search by product or brand instead.

  2. 2

    Compare with recall records

    RecallCheck checks available FDA and USDA FSIS records for matching identifiers and related product information.

  3. 3

    Verify your package

    Some recalls affect only specific lots, dates, sizes, or production codes. When those details matter, compare them with the official notice before deciding what to do.

What each result means

Recall found

RecallCheck found a current recall relationship. Follow the result instructions and compare package details with the official notice.

Check your package

A recall may apply, but a lot number, date, size, establishment number, or other package detail still needs to be verified.

No current recall match found

RecallCheck did not find the barcode in the current records checked. This is not a guarantee that the product is safe.

Older recall found

The product or barcode appears in a recall listed as closed or terminated. Older packages may still be worth comparing with the historical notice.

Why a barcode is not always enough

A barcode usually identifies a product type, not a specific production lot. A recall notice may apply only to packages made on certain dates or at a specific facility. RecallCheck shows those limitations instead of treating a barcode match as proof that every package is recalled.

Official notices are authoritative.

When RecallCheck identifies a relevant recall, use the linked FDA or USDA notice for the final package details and recommended action.
